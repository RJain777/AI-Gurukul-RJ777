A single-page, offline-capable learning portal that coaches you through AI every day — from first concepts to building agents, leading AI initiatives, and preparing for Claude certifications. It runs entirely in your browser: no login, no server, no tracking. Progress is saved on your device.

## 1. Quick start (5 minutes)

1. Open the portal. It starts on **Today**.
2. Go to **Today → Paths** and pick a path. Not sure? Start with **AI foundations — start here**. Press **Make active**.
3. Back on **Today**, press **Start today's session**. You'll see a few cards, one quiz question, and your next step.
4. For each card: say the definition out loud, press **Show answer**, then rate yourself **Again**, **Good** or **Easy**.
5. Tap any term anywhere to open its drill-down. Add your own example in **My notes**.

That's it. Ten minutes a day is the whole method.

## 2. Finding your way around

The portal has five areas along the top. Each area has a second row of sections.

| Area | Sections | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| **Today** | Today · Paths · Latest in AI | Your daily session, your path, this week's AI news |
| **Learn** | Atlas · Map · Cheat sheet · Flows | Understanding concepts and how they connect |
| **Practise** | Flashcards · Quiz · Mock interviews ↗ | Testing yourself |
| **Build** | Build lab | Hands-on guides, projects, the AIOps track and the app workshop |
| **Grow** | Lead · Certification · Roadmap | Leadership, business value, certification readiness, career plan |

**Learn — what each section does**

- **Atlas** — the AI stack as layers, from fundamentals to safety, plus a "start here" story and term chips you can tap.
- **Map** — every term as a mind map grouped by domain.
- **Cheat sheet** — every term in one scannable page, grouped and colour-coded.
- **Flows** — 15 step-by-step animated diagrams (how a prompt becomes an answer, how RAG works, how an agent loops, the AIOps loop, a full-stack app, and more). Press **Walk it step by step**.

**The drill-down** — tap any term to open a side panel with: a plain definition, an analogy, an example, the deeper detail, a one-line "say it in a meeting" version, an interview question and model answer, related terms, the guides that use it, your notes, and your review status.

**Search** — the search box (press <kbd>/</kbd>) finds everything: terms, guides, Lead sections, case studies, interview questions, flows, paths, news snapshot items, and your own notes. Each result is labelled with its type.

**Themes** — the button with three coloured dots switches between 13 themes (press <kbd>T</kbd> to cycle). Each section inside a panel has its own colour, so a page with five tables shows five colours — that's deliberate, it helps you remember where things are.

## 3. Following the learning journey

### The daily routine (10 minutes)

| Step | What happens | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Review due cards | Cards you're about to forget come back | Spaced repetition: review just before you forget |
| Learn up to 5 new cards | Taken from your active path, in order | Small daily doses stick better than cramming |
| One quiz question | A scenario linked to what you're studying | Applying a concept beats re-reading it |
| Next step | Your path's next guide, flow or checkpoint | You always know what to do next |

**How the review schedule works.** Rate each card honestly:

- **Again** — you didn't know it. It comes back tomorrow.
- **Good** — you knew it. It comes back after 3 days, then 7, 16, 35, and finally 90.
- **Easy** — you knew it instantly. It skips a step.

A wrong quiz answer automatically adds that concept back into tomorrow's review.

### The six guided paths

| Path | Steps | Typical time | For |
|---|---|---|---|
| **AI foundations — start here** | 11 | 2–3 weeks | Everyone. The vocabulary every other path builds on |
| **Claude Associate certification** | 11 | 3–4 weeks | Foundations-level credential, in study order |
| **Builder: from API to agent to app** | 16 | 6–8 weeks | Hands-on: APIs, RAG, agents, MCP, evals, a secured full-stack app |
| **Claude Architect certification** | 17 | 6–8 weeks | Orchestration, tools, context, reliability |
| **AIOps specialist** | 13 | 4–6 weeks | AI for IT operations: noise reduction, anomaly detection, ticket intelligence, safe automation |
| **AI leader** | 14 | 6–10 weeks | Initiatives, business cases, governance, leadership, interviews |

Each step is one of: a set of terms (opens a focused flashcard deck), a guide, a Lead section, a flow, or a checkpoint quiz. Steps tick themselves off: terms when you've remembered all of them, guides and Lead sections when you press **Mark as completed**, flows when you walk them to the last step, and quizzes when you score 70% or more.

### A recommended sequence

1. **AI foundations** — always first.
2. **Builder** *or* **AIOps specialist** — pick the one closest to your daily work.
3. **AI leader** — once you can explain and build, learn to lead.
4. **Claude Associate**, then **Claude Architect** — when you're ready to certify. Check readiness under **Grow → Certification**.

### A weekly rhythm

- **Daily (10 min):** the Today session.
- **Twice a week (30 min):** one guide or Lead section from your path.
- **Weekly (20 min):** one mock-interview question out loud, and one case study.
- **Weekly (5 min):** open **Latest in AI**, press **Refresh**, and tap the top theme on the trend radar.
- **Monthly:** export a backup (see section 4) and review your Roadmap.

## 4. Saving, resuming and seeing your progress

### Saving is automatic

Everything you do is saved in your browser as you go — card ratings, notes, completed guides, quiz scores, path progress, mock-interview ratings, use-case scores, ROI calculator values, theme, and where you left off. There's no save button and no account.

### Resuming

Reopen the portal on the same device and browser, and it opens on the page you left. Flashcards reopen on the same card, and the Build lab and Lead tabs reopen on the last guide or section.

### Backing up and moving to another device

Progress lives in one browser on one device. Clearing your browser data erases it, and your laptop and phone each keep their own copy. To protect it or move it:

1. Open the **⋯** menu (top right) → **Export progress (JSON)**. A file downloads.
2. On the other device or browser: **⋯** → **Import progress**, and choose that file.

Today shows a **Back up your progress** reminder if you've never exported, or if your last backup is more than 30 days old. Keep the file somewhere safe, such as OneDrive.

**⋯ → Reset progress** clears everything on this device. Export first if you might want it back.

### Several learners using the same link

Everyone can use the same GitHub Pages link. The page is the same for everyone, but **progress is stored separately in each person's own browser** — there are no accounts and nothing is stored online, so nobody can see or change anyone else's progress.

| Situation | What happens |
|---|---|
| You, a friend and your child each use your own phone or laptop | Three completely separate learning journeys. A chapter your friend completes stays incomplete for you |
| Two people share one device and one browser | They share one progress record. Give each person their own **browser profile** (Chrome: profile icon → Add; Edge: profile icon → Add profile), or their own device |
| One person, several tabs open | Tabs stay in sync automatically; changes made in different tabs are merged, so nothing is lost |
| One person, two devices (phone and laptop) | Each device has its own copy. Use **Export / Import** to move progress between them |
| Private or incognito window | Progress is discarded when the window closes |

To compare progress with friends or a class, share a screenshot of the **Today** or **Paths** page, or send an exported progress file.

### Where to see your progress

| Where | What it shows |
|---|---|
| Header, under the logo | Terms known out of 242, and your streak |
| **Today** | Cards due, new today, terms known, streak, active path % |
| **Today → Paths** | Each path's progress bar and completed steps |
| **Grow → Certification** | Readiness % for each Claude certification, based on its mapped terms |
| **Grow → Roadmap** | Ticked tasks across the five phases |
| **Build → Build lab** | Guides completed, with ticks in the list |
| **Grow → Lead** | Sections completed; self-assessment scores; use cases scored |
| **Practise → Quiz** | Recent rounds and scores |
| **Mock interviews** | Your rating per question and average per track |
| Any term's drill-down | Its next review date and level (box 1–6) |

## 5. Building your AI vocabulary — and presenting it

### The four-step vocabulary method

1. **Learn it** — the definition and analogy (Today session or Flashcards).
2. **Say it** — every term has a one-line **"In a meeting"** version. Practise saying it out loud.
3. **Own it** — write your own example from work in **My notes**. That's the version you'll remember and the one that persuades colleagues.
4. **Teach it** — explain it to someone in two minutes using the term's flow or a case study. If you can teach it, you know it.

### Preparing a 5-minute slot in a team meeting

| Minute | Content | Portal source |
|---|---|---|
| 0–1 | One concept, defined plainly | Term drill-down: definition + analogy |
| 1–3 | How it works — walk the diagram | **Flows → Walk it step by step** (share your screen) |
| 3–4 | A real example | A case study (**Grow → Lead → case studies**) or your own note |
| 4–5 | What it means for our team, plus one ask | Your note + the term's "In a meeting" line |

### Preparing a 15-minute townhall talk that motivates people

1. **Open with this week's news** (2 min) — **Latest in AI → Refresh**, then show the trend radar: "These are the themes everyone is talking about this week."
2. **One big idea, explained simply** (4 min) — pick a flow (for example, how an agent works) and walk it step by step.
3. **Two stories** (4 min) — one success and one cautionary tale from the 23 case studies. Use the **For leaders** line from each.
4. **What we're doing** (3 min) — your own project, with a number (time saved, tickets handled). A real before-and-after motivates more than any slide.
5. **How to start tomorrow** (2 min) — share the portal link and say: "Ten minutes a day. Start with the foundations path."

**Tips that motivate rather than overwhelm.** Use the "In a meeting" lines — they're written to sound confident without jargon. Show one diagram, not five. Be honest about limits: the cautionary case studies build more trust than hype. End with a small, specific first step.

## 6. Training others with this console

Everyone uses the same link; each person's progress stays on their own device.

### Four training formats

| Format | Length | Use these modules |
|---|---|---|
| **Lunch & learn series** | 6 × 30 min | One flow per session: inference, RAG, agents, MCP, evals, AIOps loop. Learners do the foundations path between sessions |
| **Six-week cohort** | 6 × 60 min + 10 min daily | Week 1–2 foundations path; week 3–4 Builder or AIOps path; week 5 Lead (use cases and ROI calculator); week 6 mock interviews and a mini-project demo |
| **One-day app workshop** | 6 sessions | **Build → App workshop**, modules 0–9, with the downloadable starter kit. The agenda is in Module 0 |
| **AI champions programme** | Monthly | Each champion runs a 5-minute slot (section 5) in their own team meeting and scores one real use case with the **use-case scorer** |

### Running a session

1. **Before:** share the portal link; ask everyone to open it on their phone and set the foundations path as active.
2. **Start (5 min):** a live quiz — open **Practise → Quiz**, share your screen, and let the room answer.
3. **Teach (15–20 min):** walk one flow step by step; open two or three terms from it.
4. **Practise (10–15 min):** pairs explain a term to each other using its "In a meeting" line; or score a team use case together in **Lead → identify and prioritise use cases**.
5. **Close (5 min):** one action — "Do today's session for the next five days."

### Measuring whether it's working

Ask learners to share a screenshot of their **Today** page or path progress each week; there's no central tracking, by design. For adoption beyond learning, use the Lead section's **ROI calculator** and **use-case scorer** with your team's real ideas.

## 7. Hosting on GitHub Pages (desktop and mobile)

Hosting the portal on GitHub Pages gives you one link that works on any laptop or phone, offline once opened.

### Files to upload (all at the top level of the repository)

| File | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `index.html` | The whole portal |
| `sw.js` | Offline support — must sit next to `index.html` |
| `manifest.webmanifest` | Lets phones install it as an app |
| `icon-192.png`, `icon-512.png` | App icons |
| `workshop-starter.zip` | The App workshop starter kit (the download button in Module 0) |
| `README.md` | This guide (optional, shows on the repository page) |
| `LICENSE.md` | The licence and credit terms — keep it in the repository |

### First-time setup (web browser only, no tools needed)

1. Sign in at github.com and choose **New repository**. Name it `AI-Gurukul-RJ777` (the name becomes part of the link, capitals included). Choose **Public** (see privacy notes below), then **Create repository**.
2. Press **uploading an existing file**, drag in all the files above, and press **Commit changes**.
3. Click **Settings** — the last tab in the row at the top of the repository (gear icon). On a narrow window it may be under a **⋯** menu at the end of the tab row.
4. In the left sidebar, under **Code and automation**, click **Pages**.
5. Under **Build and deployment** → **Source**, choose **Deploy from a branch**. Under **Branch**, change **None** to **main**, keep **/ (root)**, then press **Save**.
6. Wait one or two minutes and refresh the Pages screen. Your link appears at the top: `https://rjain777.github.io/AI-Gurukul-RJ777/` (the repository part is case-sensitive).
7. Optional: on the repository's main page, click the gear icon beside **About**, tick **Use your GitHub Pages website**, and save — the live link then shows under the description.
8. Optional but recommended: **Releases → Create a new release**, tag `v7.0`, title "AI Gurukul RJ777 v7.0", and publish. It gives the version a dated, public record.

### Using it on your phone (and offline while travelling)

- **Android (Chrome):** open the link → menu ⋮ → **Add to Home screen** / **Install app**.
- **iPhone (Safari):** open the link → Share → **Add to Home Screen**.
- Open it once while online. After that it works **offline** — on a flight, on holiday, anywhere. Only **Latest in AI → Refresh** needs a connection.
- Progress on your phone is separate from your laptop. Use **Export / Import** (section 4) to carry it across.

### Updating to a new version

Upload the new files with **Add file → Upload files** and **Commit changes**. Files with the same name are replaced — there's no need to delete anything first, and keeping the repository keeps its dated history (your public proof of authorship). Usually only `index.html` changes; when a release includes other files, upload them all together in one commit. The page checks for a new version whenever you're online, so the update appears on your next visit. Your progress is untouched, because it's stored in your browser, not in the file.

### Privacy and access

- On a free GitHub account, Pages sites come from **public** repositories, so anyone with the link can open the portal. Publishing from a private repository needs a paid GitHub plan, and even then the published page is public unless your organisation's plan supports access control.
- Your **progress, notes and scores never leave your device** — they are not in the repository.
- The portal contains no secrets. Review the **Lead → Career guidance** section before publishing, since its examples mention personal achievements.
- Some corporate networks block GitHub Pages or the APIs used by **Latest in AI**. If so, use the portal on your phone or home network, or check with your IT team.

## 8. Staying current: Latest in AI

**Today → Latest in AI** keeps your learning tied to what's happening now.

- Press **⟳ Refresh** to fetch, live from official public APIs: top AI stories this week (Hacker News), research papers today (Hugging Face Daily Papers / arXiv), trending model releases (Hugging Face), and rising AI repositories (GitHub).
- **Trusted sources only** (on by default) limits story links to about 50 reputable domains. Every link shows its domain, and trusted ones carry a ✓.
- The **trend radar** shows which themes dominate this week's headlines. Tap a theme to study the concept behind it.
- Every item has concept chips that open the related term.
- Below the live feeds: a dated snapshot of the month's highlights, and links to 14 official newsrooms and research sites — always the final word on releases.
- Results are saved, so the page still works offline, showing what you last fetched.

## 10. Troubleshooting

| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| My child's or friend's progress shows on my screen | You're sharing one browser. Give each person their own browser profile or device (see section 4) |
| My progress disappeared | It's stored per browser and per device. Check you're on the same browser. If browser data was cleared, use **Import progress** with your last backup |
| My phone shows an old version | Open the portal once while online, then close and reopen it |
| The page is blank on GitHub Pages | Check `index.html` is at the top level of the repository and Pages is set to **main / (root)**. Wait two minutes after uploading |
| It doesn't work offline | It must be opened once online after each update. Check `sw.js` sits next to `index.html` |
| A Latest in AI section shows a warning | That source was unreachable (offline, rate-limited, or blocked by your network). The other sections still load; try again later |
| The download button in the App workshop fails | Upload `workshop-starter.zip` next to `index.html` |
| A product name or limit looks out of date | Expected over time — see section 11 |

## 11. Keeping the content accurate

AI changes quickly. The portal flags this honestly:

- **Freshness flags.** Items mentioning models, prices, free plans, libraries or regulations carry a badge reading "Time-sensitive · verified October 2026". **Today → Content freshness** lists all of them, with the next re-check due January 2027.
- **Code labels.** Every code block is marked **✓ Run-tested** (executed while building the portal), **Syntax-checked** (correct code, not run against a live service), or **Prompt / template**.
- **Official sources win.** For any release, feature or certification detail, check the official newsroom or documentation linked under **Latest in AI**.

A quarterly refresh — re-checking the flagged items and updating `index.html` — keeps the portal trustworthy.
